Discuss your shipment

End-to-end lane design

The common Saudi–UAE land route crosses Al Batha on the Saudi side and Al Ghuwaifat on the UAE side. A shipment plan should reflect the collection point, final consignee, driver/carrier eligibility, operational restrictions and cargo type.

FTL, LTL and specialized cargo

Request a shipment-specific quotation for full truckload, partial loads or cargo requiring special handling. Carrier capacity, equipment suitability and border permissions are subject to confirmation.

Customs and visibility

Confirm importer-of-record arrangements, invoice and packing-list accuracy, product controls and receiving appointments before departure. Agree tracking milestones from collection through border release to delivery.

Information to include in your enquiry

Origin and destination; cargo description; quantity, weight and dimensions; delivery or storage dates; documentation requirements; and any temperature, safety or handling conditions. For chemicals or dangerous goods, include the latest safety data sheet, UN number and hazard class if applicable.

Service availability, documentation requirements and cargo acceptance are assessed for each shipment. This page is a service overview, not a guarantee of regulatory approval, transport time or warehouse acceptance.

Questions buyers ask

Is Al Batha–Al Ghuwaifat the correct border?

It is the common direct land crossing for Saudi–UAE truck movements. The final route depends on the shipment and current restrictions.

Do you guarantee a fixed transit time?

No fixed estimate can substitute for a current quotation that accounts for border processing, equipment availability and destination scheduling.

Can FLEX coordinate documents before dispatch?

Document-readiness and brokerage coordination can be included in the scope where appropriate, subject to the parties' roles and applicable laws.
